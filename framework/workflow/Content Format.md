# Content Format

This document owns the planning logic for the first three stages of the Content Lifecycle: **Trigger → Idea → Format Selection**.

It answers the question: **"Given a trigger, what content format should this become?"**

The complete Content Lifecycle (Trigger → Idea → Format Selection → Draft → Review → Final Assets → Published → Archived) is owned by `Content Pipeline.md`. This document provides the detailed rules for the planning stages within that lifecycle.

---

## Trigger-First Philosophy

Every piece of content originates from a real experience — a bug, a lecture, a conversation, a struggle, or a small win.

For the full definition of the trigger-first principle and its role in brand voice, refer to `Brand Voice.md`.

Content should never be created from abstract ideas or generic topics. A trigger is what makes content authentic and valuable. Without one, there is nothing to plan.

---

## How to Evaluate a Trigger

Not every daily experience becomes content. A good trigger has:

- **Specificity:** A specific bug, error, or moment is better than a general observation.
- **Learning outcome:** Something was learned or understood because of this trigger.
- **Shareability:** The insight can be explained clearly to a peer.
- **Relevance:** The trigger relates to the content pillars defined in `Brand View.md`.

Triggers that lack specificity or a learning outcome should be noted but not developed into content.

---

## How to Select a Format

Once a trigger is validated, choose the format based on the depth, timing, and nature of the insight.

### Carousel

Use when:
- The concept requires a structured, multi-slide explanation that guides the reader step by step.
- The reader needs to follow a learning journey, not just see a conclusion.
- The insight has enough depth to fill 5–10 slides grounded in real code or project work.

Refer to `Carousels.md` for full format rules — structure, slide schema, constraints, and best practices.

### Reel

Use when:
- A single insight can be delivered in under 60 seconds.
- The learning environment (IDE, terminal, desk, campus) adds value to the message.
- The hook is a mistake, struggle, or visual contrast that drives curiosity.

Refer to `Reels.md` for full format rules — authenticity, recording expectations, pacing, and structure.

### Story

Use when:
- The moment is ephemeral and does not need to be preserved permanently.
- The content is a quick update, WIP loop, behind-the-scenes glimpse, or low-friction interaction.
- The goal is presence and familiarity rather than depth.

Refer to `Stories.md` for full format rules — categories, unpolished standards, engagement mechanics.

## Distribution Philosophy

The distribution balance below is a guide, not a strict rule. It reflects the student-builder journey where learning documentation is the primary output, with room for personal reflection and project updates.

### Carousels (Primary Long-Form Format)
- Educational (I learned X): 50%
- Education + Personal Opinion (Why I like X): 25%
- Project Thinking (How I solved X): 15%
- Project Documentation (What I built today): 10%

### Reels (Quick Video Format)
- Quick Educational (Mistake / Tip): 50%
- Personal Reflection (Student life / Thinking): 30%
- Project Related (IDE / Terminal clips): 20%

### Stories (Daily Presence Format)
- Learning Journey (WIP Loops): 40%
- Behind the Scenes (Unpolished 20%): 25%
- Quick Value (Small tips): 20%
- Engagement (Low-friction polls): 10%
- Project Updates (Real wins): 5%

### Goals of Every Content Piece

Regardless of format, every piece should aim to:

- Document learning authentically.
- Build trust by showing the real struggle.
- Show progress over perfection.
- Start conversations at a peer level.

## Relationship to the Content Lifecycle

The planning decisions in this document correspond to the **Trigger, Idea, and Format Selection** stages of the Content Lifecycle defined in `Content Pipeline.md`.

`Content Format.md` answers only **"what format should this become?"** It does not describe how drafts are written, how visuals are created, how content is reviewed, or how it is published. Those production stages — Draft, Review, Final Assets, Published, Archived — are owned by `Content Pipeline.md`.
