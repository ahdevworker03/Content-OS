# Brand Voice

This document is the Single Source of Truth for all writing voice, tone, and stylistic rules. Every content piece — regardless of format or platform — must align with the voice and style defined here.

Content decisions about what to post are owned by `Brand View.md` (creator identity, audience, content boundaries, content pillars). This document owns _how_ the creator writes and sounds.

---

## Communication Personality

- A Lebanese Computer Science student learning in public
- Honest, grounded, and reflective
- Professional in structure, never authoritative in tone
- Comfortable with uncertainty and learning-in-progress
- Relatable and experience-driven
- Clear and simple, never unnecessarily complex

---

## Voice Attributes

- **Clear and grounded** — Avoid jargon unless technically necessary. When used, explain naturally within context.
- **Honest student perspective** — Speak from experience, not from expertise. Use "I discovered," "I struggled with," "I learned" rather than "you should," "the right way is."
- **Reflective, not instructional** — Document what happened and what it taught you. Do not lecture or prescribe.
- **Comfortable with uncertainty** — Use phrases like "I'm still figuring this out," "this is what I understand so far," "I might be wrong but."
- **Anchored in real student life** — Reference exams, deadlines, stress, university workload, and real constraints when naturally relevant.

---

## Tonal Range

The core tone is reflective and experience-driven, but it adapts across contexts:

| Context                                 | Tone                                            |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Core carousels (educational deep-dives) | Reflective, structured, clarity-focused         |
| Debugging stories                       | Frustrated → curious → relieved (narrative arc) |
| Daily updates / stories                 | Casual, conversational, unpolished              |
| LinkedIn reflections                    | Professional, polished, structured              |
| Celebrations (shipping a feature)       | Enthusiastic but humble                         |

In all contexts: never authoritative, never generic, never purely theoretical.

---

## Writing Style Guidelines

- Prioritize clarity over complexity or depth for its own sake.
- Avoid teaching tone; prioritize reflection and documentation.
- Show process and reasoning, not only conclusions.
- Use simple sentence structures. Favor short paragraphs.
- Lead with the trigger (the bug, the confusion, the question) before explaining the resolution.
- Keep language conversational — write how you would explain to a peer, not to a class.
- For Arabic content: write in conversational Lebanese Arabic, integrating English technical terms naturally.
- For English content: use clear, professional language with a reflective tone.

---

## Dos and Don'ts

### Do

- Start every piece from a real experience or trigger.
- Use first-person perspective throughout.
- Acknowledge mistakes, confusion, and wrong turns.
- Connect insights to the specific implementation or problem that taught them.
- Keep language natural and conversational.
- Include student-life context when it adds to the narrative.

### Don't

- Teach or lecture as if from a position of authority.
- Use generic motivational statements.
- Post abstract theory without a real-world trigger.
- Write in a polished, "perfect developer" voice.
- Over-explain or add complexity for its own sake.
- Use a robotic or overly formal tone.

## Platform Rules

These rules define how the core brand voice adapts to each platform. They describe adaptation — not redefinition. Every platform still follows the Communication Personality, Voice Attributes, and Tonal Range defined above.

### Instagram

**Purpose**
Primary platform for daily learning documentation, visual storytelling, and community building. Instagram is where the student journey is documented in real time through carousels, reels, and stories.

**Target communication style**
Conversational Lebanese Arabic with English technical terms embedded naturally. The writing should feel like talking to a peer who shares the same learning context.

**Expected level of depth**
Medium as a baseline, with range depending on the chosen format. Per-format depth expectations are defined in Carousels.md, Reels.md, and Stories.md — this section defines platform-level adaptation only.

**Tone adaptation**
Close to the core reflective tone with a conversational feel. Per-format tone adaptation is defined in Carousels.md, Reels.md, and Stories.md. Never authoritative.

**Audience expectations**
Arabic-speaking CS students across the Arab world. They expect authentic, experience-based learning content, not tutorials. They value the shared struggle of learning.

**Language usage**
Lebanese Arabic as the primary language. English technical terms (API, bug, deploy, framework, function) are preserved in their original form and embedded naturally within Arabic sentences. Code snippets remain in English.

**Appropriate call-to-actions**
Low-friction community CTAs: "What part of this is still confusing?" "Have you run into this before?" Use polls and reaction sliders in stories. Avoid generic "like and follow" requests.

---

### LinkedIn

**Purpose**
Professional platform for structured reflections and career-oriented documentation. LinkedIn is where the same learning experiences are framed as professional growth narratives.

**Target communication style**
English only. Professional but never corporate. Structured and polished while maintaining the student perspective.

**Expected level of depth**
Medium-to-high. LinkedIn posts expand on the same ideas shared on Instagram but with more structured reasoning, professional framing, and emphasis on growth and process.

**Tone adaptation**
More polished and structured than Instagram. The reflective tone remains but is presented with professional framing. Still student-perspective, never expert-perspective. Less casual, more deliberate.

**Audience expectations**
Professional network, fellow developers, potential employers, and recruiters. They expect thoughtful reflections on real engineering work, not self-promotion.

**Language usage**
English exclusively. Technical terms remain in English context. Avoid Lebanese Arabic entirely. The translation from the Instagram version should be a rewrite, not a machine translation — preserve the insight and experience, restructure the delivery.

**Appropriate call-to-actions**
Discussion-oriented CTAs: "What has your experience been with this?" "What approach do you prefer?" Engage with comments thoughtfully. Avoid sales-oriented CTAs.

---

### X (Twitter)

**Purpose**
Micro-reflection platform for quick technical insights, learning-in-public snippets, and community interaction. X is where small discoveries and immediate reactions live.

**Target communication style**
Concise and direct. Lebanese Arabic with English technical terms. Every post should deliver a single insight or observation in minimal words.

**Expected level of depth**
Low. X is for micro-content — one idea per post, no long-form development. Threads are acceptable for slightly deeper topics but must remain tight.

**Tone adaptation**
Casual and conversational, closest to the unpolished end of the tonal range. Quick reactions, honest frustrations, immediate takeaways. Less structured than any other platform.

**Audience expectations**
Developer community and tech Twitter. They expect quick, genuine value — a tip, a hot take, a relatable struggle. Brevity and authenticity are valued over comprehensiveness.

**Language usage**
Lebanese Arabic primary, with English technical terms preserved. Code snippets and technical references remain in English. Threads in mixed language follow the same pattern — Arabic narrative with English terminology.

**Appropriate call-to-actions**
Engagement-focused: questions, polls, "what do you think?" replies. Retweet-worthy single-insight posts. Threads that invite discussion. Avoid hard sells.

---

### Stories

**Purpose**
Daily presence and familiarity building. Stories document the unfiltered, behind-the-scenes reality of a student learning in public. They are the lowest-friction content format.

**Target communication style**
Unpolished, minimal editing, in-the-moment. Lebanese Arabic with occasional English technical terms. The writing should feel like a voice note in text form.

**Expected level of depth**
Low. Stories are ephemeral and intentionally shallow. Quick updates, WIP loops, and casual observations. Save depth for carousels and LinkedIn.

**Tone adaptation**
Closest to raw authenticity. Frustrations are okay. Confusion is expected. The tonal arc for debugging stories (frustrated → curious → relieved) is the primary narrative structure. No polish, no perfection.

**Audience expectations**
Followers who want the real, uncurated version of the learning journey. They expect to see the messy desk, the broken code, the exam stress, and the small wins that never make it to a carousel.

**Language usage**
Lebanese Arabic primary. English technical terms preserved naturally. Stories are the most language-mixed format — they reflect how the creator actually thinks and speaks day-to-day.

**Appropriate call-to-actions**
Low-friction interactions only: polls, reaction sliders, this-or-that choices. Replace open-ended question boxes with binary options. No sales CTAs, no link-in-bio pushes unless tied to a specific carousel drop.

## Language Strategy

This section defines permanent framework guidance on language usage across all platforms.

### Primary Language by Platform

| Platform    | Primary Language | Secondary Use           |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ----------------------- |
| Instagram   | Lebanese Arabic  | English technical terms |
| LinkedIn    | English          | None                    |
| X (Twitter) | Lebanese Arabic  | English technical terms |
| Stories     | Lebanese Arabic  | English technical terms |

### English Technical Terms — When to Preserve

Preserve English terms when:

- The term has no widely recognized Arabic equivalent (e.g., API, function, framework, deployment, query, endpoint, middleware).
- The term is a programming language, library, or tool name (e.g., Python, React, Node.js, Git, VS Code, Linux).
- The term appears in code snippets or terminal output — code is always in English regardless of the surrounding language.
- The term is more precise or widely understood in English than any Arabic alternative.

Translate when:

- A clear, natural Arabic alternative exists and is commonly used in Arab developer communities.
- The concept is part of general computing education (e.g., "database" → "قاعدة بيانات" when used generically).
- Using English would create unnecessary friction for the primary audience.

### Consistency Expectations

- Within a single piece of content, language choice must be consistent. Do not switch between Lebanese Arabic and English prose mid-sentence unless the English is a technical term.
- The same idea, when adapted across platforms, must be rewritten — not translated. An Instagram carousel in Arabic and a LinkedIn post in English are different pieces of content that share the same insight.
- Platform language strategy is fixed. Instagram content is always primarily Arabic. LinkedIn content is always English. Do not mix strategies.

### Language Mixing Rules

- Acceptable mixing: Arabic narrative with English technical terms, English code snippets, English project/tool names.
- Unacceptable mixing: Switching prose language mid-sentence for non-technical words. Mixing Arabic and English in the same LinkedIn post. Using English where a natural Arabic alternative exists for general concepts.
- Code and technical references: Always English, always preserved verbatim.
- Audience interactions (comments, replies, DMs): Match the audience's language. Respond in Arabic on Instagram, English on LinkedIn, Arabic on X.

## Platform Rewriting Rules

These rules define how a single content idea is transformed for different platforms. The objective is to prevent copy-pasting content between platforms while maintaining brand consistency.

### What Remains Consistent Across Platforms

- **Core insight**: The underlying learning, discovery, or experience is the same across all platforms.
- **Brand voice attributes**: Honest, student perspective, reflective, experience-driven, clear and grounded.
- **Trigger-first principle**: Every piece of content originates from a real trigger regardless of platform.
- **First-person perspective**: Always "I" language, never "you should" or authoritative framing.
- **No generic content**: No platform receives generic, triggerless content.

### What Changes Per Platform

| Dimension     | Instagram                          | LinkedIn                     | X (Twitter)           | Stories                       |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Language**  | Lebanese Arabic                    | English                      | Lebanese Arabic       | Lebanese Arabic               |
| **Depth**     | Medium (format-dependent)          | Medium-to-high               | Low                   | Low                           |
| **Structure** | Visual slide sequence              | Text post with paragraphs    | Single post or thread | Photo/video with minimal text |
| **Hook**      | Visual or question-driven          | Insight or reflection-driven | Punchy observation    | In-the-moment reaction        |
| **CTA**       | Community question                 | Discussion starter           | Poll / reply prompt   | Binary interaction            |
| **Polish**    | Varies by format — per-format polish levels defined in Carousels.md, Reels.md, and Stories.md | High (polished reflections)  | Low (quick thoughts)  | Lowest (raw, unpolished)      |

### How Messaging Should Be Adapted

- Start with the same core trigger and insight.
- For Instagram: Lead with the visual hook. Build the narrative through slides. Use Arabic narrative structure.
- For LinkedIn: Lead with the professional takeaway. Frame the same experience as career growth or engineering reflection. Write in English.
- For X: Extract the single most interesting angle from the idea. Deliver it in one sentence. Use a thread only if the idea genuinely requires more space.
- For Stories: Show the raw version of the trigger — the broken code, the confusion. Minimal text, maximum authenticity.

### How Depth, Wording, Hooks, CTAs, and Structure Vary

**Depth variation:**

- A carousel idea gets its full depth on its primary platform. Carousel structure (slide count, sequence) is defined in Carousels.md.
- The same idea on LinkedIn becomes a 3–4 paragraph reflection with professional framing.
- On X it becomes a 1–2 post insight thread at most.
- In Stories it becomes a 30-second WIP loop with one sentence of context.

**Wording variation:**

- Instagram wording: Arabic narrative, conversational, "I found" logic.
- LinkedIn wording: English, more deliberate, "This experience taught me" framing.
- X wording: Arabic, tight, one thought per post.
- Stories wording: Minimal Arabic, whatever captures the moment.

**Hook variation:**

- Instagram: "I spent 2 hours debugging this and here is what I found" with a visual hook. Per-format hook patterns are defined in Carousels.md and Reels.md.
- LinkedIn: "This week, a bug forced me to rethink how I approach [concept]" — professional, reflective opener.
- X: "Debugged [issue] for 2 hours. Turns out I was [mistake]." — direct, no setup.
- Stories: Show the broken screen or error. Text: "مش عارف شو المشكلة" (I don't know what the problem is).

**CTA variation:**

- Instagram: "What part of this is still confusing for you?" — primary CTA pattern for carousel format. Format-specific CTA details are defined in Carousels.md.
- LinkedIn: "Have you faced a similar challenge? What helped you?"
- X: "What's your take on this?"
- Stories: Poll: "تعبت" (exhausted) vs "لسا طازة" (still fresh)

**Structure variation:**

- Instagram: visual narrative flow. The Instagram carousel structure (slide sequence, count) is defined in Carousels.md.
- LinkedIn: Opening insight → Context/trigger → Reflection → Lesson → Discussion question (paragraphs).
- X: One post = one idea. Thread = connected sequence of single-idea posts.
- Stories: Single frame or short sequence. No structure beyond the moment.

## Mandatory Output Package

This section defines the deliverables expected after developing a content idea. The pipeline and AI prompts should reference this section for output expectations.

### Required Outputs

For every content idea that enters production, the following must be produced:

1. **Primary content piece** — At least one complete, publishable piece of content in the selected format (carousel, reel, LinkedIn post, or X post).
2. **Platform-adapted version** — For every target platform, a version that follows the Platform Rewriting Rules above. If an idea targets both Instagram and LinkedIn, both versions must exist.
3. **Core insight statement** — A one-sentence summary of the trigger and insight that the content is built on. This anchors all platform variants to the same idea.

### Optional Outputs

The following may be produced depending on capacity and relevance:

1. **Secondary platform variants** — Additional platform versions beyond the primary targets (e.g., an X thread derived from an Instagram carousel).
2. **Story content** — One or more story posts derived from the same idea, particularly for WIP loops or behind-the-scenes content.
3. **Alternative format versions** — The same insight converted to a different format (e.g., a reel derived from a carousel's hook).
4. **Archive metadata** — Structured metadata for the content archive (platform, pillar, format, tags, publishing date).

### Relationship Between Primary and Secondary Content

- The primary content piece determines the format and depth anchor. Secondary content adapts from that anchor — it does not redefine it.
- Secondary content is derived, not duplicated. Each variant must be a genuine adaptation following the Platform Rewriting Rules.
- Optional outputs are never required. They are produced only when they add value without reducing the quality of the primary content.
- Story content can be produced independently of primary content. Not every story needs a carousel or post to support it.

### Deliverable Format Expectations

| Output Type            | Format                          | Content                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Instagram carousel     | Slide sequence (text + visuals) | Lebanese Arabic. Carousel structure (slide count, sequence) defined in Carousels.md |
| Instagram reel         | Video clip(s) with caption      | Lebanese Arabic caption, English code visuals       |
| LinkedIn post          | Text post                       | English, reflective, professional structure         |
| X post / thread        | Text post(s)                    | Lebanese Arabic, concise, single-insight            |
| Story                  | Photo/video with text overlay   | Lebanese Arabic, minimal text, raw moment           |
| Core insight statement | Single sentence                 | Trigger → Insight summary in one line               |
