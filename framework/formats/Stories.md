# Stories

Stories are for daily presence and building familiarity.
They should feel unpolished, interactive, and grounded in a student's day.

Stories should feel like:

- A real student's day (including non-coding moments)
- A real learning journey
- A behind-the-scenes view (messy desk included)
- A casual but intentional update

For writing voice, tone, and platform adaptation, refer to `Brand Voice.md`.

---

## What Unpolished Means

Unpolished means the content is posted as it happened — no editing, no retakes, no curated framing.

- **Acceptable spontaneity:** A photo of the desk mid-session, a screenshot of an error, a quick selfie after a breakthrough, a video pan across the terminal.
- **Acceptable imperfections:** Bad lighting, cluttered background, crooked framing, typos in text overlay, unfinished sentences, background noise.
- **Acceptable frequency:** Daily when possible, but skip days are normal. Multiple stories on eventful days are welcome.
- **What makes a story feel authentic:** The content is clearly of the moment. It could not have been planned or scheduled.

---

## Boundaries

Unpolished does not mean careless. The line between authentic and careless:

- A story is authentic when it captures a real moment. A story is careless when it wastes the viewer's time (blurry footage of nothing, incoherent rambling, content without a point).
- Do not post anything that would require an apology or explanation later.
- Privacy boundaries still apply. Do not show other people without consent, sensitive information, or personal locations.
- If you would not share it with a peer sitting next to you, it does not belong in a story.

---

## Temporary Nature

Stories disappear after 24 hours. This changes what belongs in a story versus other formats.

- Stories are safe for throwaway content: quick reactions, imperfect sharing, half-formed thoughts.
- Do not treat stories as drafts for permanent posts. A story is a moment, not a rehearsal.
- If a story insight is valuable enough to preserve, rewrite it as a carousel or post with proper structure and depth.
- The temporary nature lowers the bar for posting. Use this to share more, not less.

---

## Engagement Style

Stories use the lowest-friction interaction tools by design.

- Replace open-ended question boxes with binary choices: polls, reaction sliders, this-or-that stickers.
- The goal is participation, not depth. A tap on a poll is success.
- Respond to replies in the same casual, unpolished tone. Story conversations should feel like texting, not customer support.

## 1) Learning Journey & WIP Loops

### Purpose

Show what I am currently struggling with or learning.

### What to post

- **WIP Loops:** Show the "ugly" UI or the broken terminal screen before the fix.
- What topic I am studying right now.
- The "I don't know yet" moments.
- What finally made sense after a long session.

---

## 2) Unpolished 20% (The Human Side)

### Purpose

Build familiarity through non-technical student life.

### What to post

- Coffee runs / Study snacks
- Desk setup (clean or messy)
- Library sessions
- Exam prep / University stress
- Laptop stickers / Terminal themes

## 3) Quick Value

### Purpose

Share a tiny lesson from the day's learning.

---

## 4) Interaction (Friction Reduction)

### Purpose

Create easy engagement with fellow students.

### Rule

Replace open-ended Question Boxes with binary/low-friction stickers:
- **Polls:** "React or Vanilla JS?", "Vim or VS Code?"
- **Reaction Sliders:** "How much do you hate CSS today?"
- **This or That:** Simple tech/study choices.

---

## 5) Project Updates

### Purpose

Share small wins from the Car Rental System.
Only post when the update is real — don't force it.

### Constraint

Project updates in stories must show the change visually. A screenshot, a screen recording, or a photo of the terminal. Text-only project updates belong in posts.
