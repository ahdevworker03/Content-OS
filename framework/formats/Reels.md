# Reels

Reels are short, fast video glimpses into the learning process.
They capture authentic learning moments in motion — quick, visual, and grounded in the actual environment.

## What Makes Reels Unique

Reels are the only format that shows the learning environment in motion. Unlike carousels (structured slide-by-slide explanation) or stories (ephemeral presence), reels deliver a single insight in under 60 seconds with visual proof — the IDE, the terminal, the desk, the campus.

Reels are best for:

- Quick reflections
- Small technical insights
- Light project updates
- "Facepalm" hooks that drive curiosity

For writing voice, tone, and platform adaptation, refer to `Brand Voice.md`.

---

## Authenticity in Reels

Reel authenticity comes from showing the real environment, not from production polish.

- The learning environment must be visible: IDE, terminal, desk, campus, or library. A reel filmed against a blank wall or in a staged setup breaks the format.
- Natural imperfections are expected and welcome: background noise, pauses, mid-sentence corrections, imperfect lighting.
- The value is in the moment, not the editing. Minimal cuts are better than polished transitions.
- What makes a reel feel authentic: real reactions to bugs or discoveries, actual code on screen, genuine frustration or excitement.
- Do not reenact or stage a learning moment. If it did not happen naturally during recording, it does not belong in a reel.

---

## Recording Expectations

- Record in a single take when possible. Multi-clip reels should feel like a sequence of real moments, not a scripted production.
- At least one clip must be in a "student" setting — campus, messy desk, library, or cafe. The environment is part of the content.
- Acceptable: pauses, restarts, background noise, reflections on screen, cluttered desk, casual clothing.
- Not acceptable: staged reactions, scripted dialogue, completely dark or blurry footage, inaudible audio, pretending to work.

---

## Pacing

- Reels must deliver one insight in under 60 seconds. If the insight requires more time, it is a carousel or post, not a reel.
- The hook must land within the first 2–3 seconds. A mistake, a struggle, or a visual contrast works best.
- Each reel has exactly one focus. Multiple insights require multiple reels.
- Pacing matches the content: quick tips work with fast cuts, reflections should breathe with longer clips.

---

## 1) Quick Insights

### Purpose

Deliver one small thing I learned quickly.

### Structure

- **Hook:** Start with a mistake or struggle (e.g., "I spent 3 hours on this bug so you don't have to").
- **Show, don't tell:** Display the actual IDE, terminal window, or physical setup. Text-on-screen alone does not work for reels.
- **Close:** One sentence takeaway. No elaborate conclusions.

### Possible ideas

- One programming tip I found
- One Linux tip from my setup
- One Git trick I finally got
- One tool I'm using
- One beginner mistake I made
- One concept I finally understood in under a minute

---

## 2) Personal Reflection

### Purpose

Show my perspective in a short, unpolished format.

### What to do

- Share something I realized
- Talk about a mistake I made this week
- Share a thought about university or exam stress
- Show progress in my thinking

### Constraints

- Keep the reflection tied to a specific moment. Generic thoughts belong in carousels or posts, not reels.
- The environment should still be visible. A reflection filmed at a desk or on campus connects better than a close-up face shot with a blank background.

---

## 3) Project Clips

### Purpose

Show movement in the project in a short visual format.

### Content style

- Visual and simple
- Focus on the IDE and terminal
- Show "Before" (Broken/Ugly) and "After" (Fixed/Better)
- Progress-oriented

### Constraint

- The change must be visible on screen. Abstract progress (e.g., "I refactored the backend") without visual proof does not work for reels.

---

## What Reels Should Never Become

Reels break when they try to look produced. Never use:

- Scripted voiceovers or studio audio
- Artificial lighting setups or professional staging
- Reenacted learning moments that did not happen during recording
- Multiple reshoots to get the "perfect" take

If a moment is worth sharing but requires production, it belongs in a carousel or a LinkedIn post — not a reel.
