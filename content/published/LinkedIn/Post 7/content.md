# During my first year of Computer Science, I learned that starting a portfolio matters more than perfecting it.

During my first year of Computer Science, I learned that starting a portfolio matters more than perfecting it.

At first, I almost didn't build one. I thought I needed to know more — React, TypeScript, deployment, animations. I told myself I'd build it once I was "ready."

But when I started applying for training opportunities, I realized something: a CV alone wasn't enough. What made a difference was having something tangible to show — a live website that proved I could build.

So I built it. Simple. Static. HTML, CSS, and a bit of JavaScript.

Here's what that experience taught me:

**1. Building taught me more than studying.**
Applying basic web technologies in a real, deployed project taught me more about HTML/CSS/JS than any tutorial ever did.

**2. Having something to show changed the outcome.**
When I applied for trainings, I wasn't just saying "I know this" — I was showing my work. That opened doors my CV alone couldn't.

**3. Starting early matters more than being perfect.**
My portfolio wasn't a masterpiece. It was a static site. But it existed. And that made all the difference.

What I learned most of all: waiting until I felt ready would have meant never starting. The portfolio I built in my first year taught me more than the perfect one I was waiting to build.
